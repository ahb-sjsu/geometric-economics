**CONSENT NOTICE**

**TITLE OF STUDY**

Choices about points for you and for a charity

**NAME OF RESEARCHER**

Andrew H. Bond, [title], [department], San José State University

**PURPOSE**

This study is about how people choose when a choice affects both themselves and a charity, and when some outcomes
depend on a coin flip. Researchers use mathematical models to describe how people value options like these. This study
compares several of these models, to learn which one best predicts people's choices.

**PROCEDURES**

The study takes about 42 minutes, in one sitting, on this web page. You will choose one of five charities, read the
rules, and answer four questions about them. Then you will make 160 choices. Each choice is between two options, and
each option gives points to you and points to the charity you chose. Some options are sure, and some depend on a coin
flip. A few choices check attention, and a few appear twice. At the end you will answer four short questions about the
study. The last one is optional.

**RISKS AND BENEFITS**

The risks are no greater than those of everyday computer use. Some people may find the choices repetitive. You may not
benefit directly, apart from the payment. The results may help researchers understand choices that affect other
people.

**INCENTIVES AND COMPENSATION**

You will receive $8.40 through Prolific for taking part. At the end, the computer draws one of your choices at random,
and that choice is paid for real, at 5 cents a point. Your points from it are paid to you as a bonus on Prolific, up to
$5.00. The charity's points from it are donated to the charity, up to $5.00. If you chose a coin-flip option in that
choice, the computer flips a fair coin to decide which of its two outcomes is paid.

If you do not answer the four questions about the rules correctly within two tries, the study ends there, and you
still receive the $8.40. Your answers to the attention checks do not change your payment. If you stop before the end,
you will not be paid. If a technical problem stops you, send the researcher a message on Prolific, and you will
receive the $8.40.

The researcher makes the donations after the study, one payment to each charity, and publishes the receipts at
[receipts address].

**CONFIDENTIALITY**

The study records your Prolific ID, the charity you choose, your answers, your choices and how long each one takes,
the times you start and finish, and the size of your browser window. It also records how many times you reload the
page, and how many times you leave it during the choices. It does not ask for your name or contact details, and it
does not record your IP address. The services that host this page and pass on your answers keep their own technical
logs, which the researcher cannot see.

Your answers are saved as you go. A service called DataPipe passes them to a private folder in San José State
University's Google storage, which only the researcher can open. Your web browser also keeps a copy, so that you can
continue if the page reloads. You can remove it by clearing your browser's data for this site.

Before the data are analyzed, your Prolific ID is replaced by a code. The file linking the code to your Prolific ID is
kept private by the researcher. Files that contain your Prolific ID are deleted three years after the study ends.
Prolific knows who you are, but the researcher does not.

Results are published without Prolific IDs. Your coded choices may be shared in a public research archive, so that
other researchers can check and reuse them. They will not include your Prolific ID or anything you write in the
optional question.

**YOUR RIGHTS**

Your participation in this study is completely voluntary. You can refuse to take part, or stop at any time by closing
this page and returning the study on Prolific, without any negative effect on your relations with San José State
University. To move on, each choice and question needs an answer, except the last question at the end, which is
optional. Until the study closes on Prolific, you can ask for your data to be deleted by sending the researcher a
message on Prolific.
